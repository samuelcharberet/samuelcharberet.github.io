The Dynamic Energy Budget theory has been introduced by S.A.L.M. Kooijman in the 90'. This is a theory, which means it is a set of equations that are supposed to describe reality. Kooijman describes it in his book as <i>the best tested quantitative theory in biology</i> {% cite kooijman2009 marques2018 %}. So this it sounds like this theory should be known and used by everyone ! However, few of my colleagues actually know it and in my current lab, no one has ever published on it. But a general theory of what life is sounds like something we should try to learn and understand. This is how I started reading {% cite kooijman2009 %}. As a first note, I find a bit surprising that for a theory whith such high claims, there is no collective textbooks, but only one book written by the creator of the theory himself. 

# About the name "DEB"

The name of the theory, dynamic energy budgets, might rebuke some. It is a bit complicated. In my opinion, it does not convey well what the theory does. The "dynamic" seem to refer to the fact that the model accounts for changes in the state of the organism as it grows. So here, maybe the word "ontogenic" would be adapted as well. 
\\
\\
As for the "energy" part, it raises a lot of questions as to what is here defined as energy. And this might be oe of the highest obstacle to the actual sucess of the theory as measured by how many people use it. Do you know of an experimentalist who works with energy ? Who, in the biological sciences research, measures the "energy" of something ? Very few people. Most people measure concentrations, masses, number of deaths, RNA expression. I believe that this is because these variable became available, and the energetic aspects of living organism, are both vague and hard to measure. The use of energy stems from the conservation law, which make this variable easier to track mathematically. However, there are also two other variables, which are conserved in biological processes: mass and entropy. Among these three, mass is, by far, the easiest to measure for biological systems.
