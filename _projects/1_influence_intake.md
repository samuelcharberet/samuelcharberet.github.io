Consumers like herbivores mediate nutrient fluxes through food webs via ingestion, growth, and waste production. The balance among these individual-level processes is expected to scale up and shape nutrient fluxes and trophic transfer at the ecosystem level. During my PhD, I quantitatively assessed how food limitation during growth affects the nutrients' fate after ingestion, i.e. whether food consumed would transformed into bodymass, waste or respiration depending on the food available. We estimated the balance of eight elements in larvae of Spodoptera littoralis, including the major nutrients C, N, P but also some other nutrients, also important for ecology (K, S, Ca, Mg, Na).
\\
\\
The efficiency of assimilation, i.e. the proportion of nutrients that does not end up as waste decreased with intake rate for all nutrients. It was already known that the bulk (i.e. the sum of all nurients) biomass assimilation efficiency often decreases when intake rate is high. This is often understood as an effect of higher gut passage rate, which limits digestion completeness. Our analysis showed that this is also true for particular nutrients. But the intake rate also had an effect on the nutrients proportions in the body of our larvae. The larvae feeding at low intake rates were richer in elements other than carbon. And intake rate also had an effect on the relative proportions of nutrients in the frass (the waste thatinsect produce, which is a mixture of faeces and urine). This is important because frass can often be generated in large amounts uring insect blooms, and its "quality" (the relative proportion of nutrients) will affect the decomposition rate of these waste. In turn, this can affect plant growth. In this case, larvae feeding at low intake produced frass with lower quality, in addition to producing ew of it. This effect of intake on waste quality was beforehand not documented.
\\
\\
An interesting feature of the effect of intake on mass budgets is when we consider growth efficiency. This variable measures the proportion of food ingested that ends up being new body tissue. The growth efficiency peaked at intermediate value of both intake rate and growth rate. This is interesting because this suggests that growth efficiency and growth rate can not be maximised simultaneously. There is a rich literature on the tradeoff between rate and yield, or between power and efficiency which this is linked to. As I was navigating my data and the literature on this subject, I started being deeply interested in the deeper connection between rate and yield, which I know think is a fundamental constraint on the dynamics of living system.
